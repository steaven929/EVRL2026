ABSTRACT
As an intelligent mobility strategy, Electric Vehicle (EV) relocation plays crucial roles in improving system-wide profitability and service efficiency. However, coordinating vehicle relocation and charging under dynamic demand and constrained infrastructure poses significant challenges. To address this issue, this paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning-based charging relocation framework that integrates a Markov decision process with the deep deterministic policy gradient algorithm. The EV relocation problem is modeled as a continuous decision-making process with dual objectives: relocating idle vehicles to fulfill customer demands and navigating vehicles to charging facilities based on battery conditions. The action space is defined by each vehicle’s departure inclination, while the state space captures key system-level features, including zonal demand volume, parking availability, and charger usage status. A composite reward function is designed to guide policy learning, with rewards for demand completion and charging behavior, and penalties for battery depletion. A simulation environment is constructed using real-world travel demand and charging infrastructure data from Nanjing, China, covering four parking zones. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves vehicle utilization and system profit, outperforming conventional rule-based strategies, the learned policy reduces the number of out-of-battery events by 42.86%. This study provides a scalable and adaptive solution for real-time scheduling in EV-sharing systems under uncertain and dynamic conditions.
Keywords: Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Shared Autonomous Electric Vehicle
INTRODUCTION
With the rapid development of industrialization, energy consumption has significant increase, and people's lifestyles have largely changed. However, this process has also led to serious environmental problems due to the heavy reliance on fossil fuels. Climate change caused by greenhouse gas emissions has reached a dangerous level, requiring many preventive actions (1). In response, the International Energy Agency (IEA) has outlined a vision for future energy systems aimed at limiting the rise in global average temperature to below 2 degrees Celsius by 2050 (2). To curb the rising trend of global temperatures, it is essential to reduce greenhouse gas emissions at the source. Clean energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydropower produce little to no greenhouse gas emissions during electricity generation, making them green alternatives to fossil fuels. A large-scale shift toward clean energy can significantly reduce carbon emissions, promote energy structure transformation, and support sustainable development.
In urban transportation systems, conventional fuel vehicles rely on gasoline or diesel during operation. Their internal combustion engines emit large amounts of carbon dioxide and nitrogen oxides when burning fossil fuels. These emissions not only worsen the greenhouse effect but also lead to serious urban air pollution. In contrast, Electric Vehicles (EVs) powered by zero- or low-carbon electricity from renewable sources are considered a highly promising alternative to fuel-based vehicles. With advantages in environmental protection and energy efficiency, EVs are widely regarded as a necessary trend for reducing carbon emissions and decreasing dependence on fossil fuels (3). As a result, their market penetration is increasing at an unprecedented rate, gradually replacing conventional fuel vehicles and serving as a key force in decarbonizing the transportation system (4). EVs offer many advantages, including high energy efficiency, reduced emissions, and lower operating costs. In addition, with the advancement of battery technology and the improvement of charging infrastructure, the driving range of EVs is increasing, and their convenience is improving. EVs also have strong integration capabilities with intelligent systems (5). They can work closely with autonomous driving technologies and Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) systems, making them a key component of intelligent transportation systems.
One-way station-based vehicle sharing has gradually emerged as a promising mode of transportation. Shared mobility refers to the transportation services and resources that are shared among users, either concurrently or one after another. This concept encompasses a wide range of modes including ride-hailing, car sharing, bike sharing, scooter sharing, and shared autonomous vehicles. EV fleets are frequently deployed in shared mobility systems to support sustainable and efficient transportation. This mode offers flexibility to users without the private car purchase cost. It also has the potential to reduce vehicle ownership, ease urban traffic congestion. However, deploying shared EV fleets also brings several challenges. First, EVs are constrained by charging time, charger availability, and battery capacity. EV charging behavior is affected by many uncertain factors, such as State of Charge (SOC), user demand, traffic conditions, charging station utilization, and electricity prices. EVs must consider whether parking locations are equipped with chargers and whether the charging process is convenient and time-efficient. Long charging times limit vehicle availability, which in turn compromises the overall user experience. Moreover, it is crucial for the system to be responsive and scalable in real time. The randomness and concentration of charging activities may lead to efficiency loss, local congestion, and energy waste.
Moreover, traditional scheduling methods often rely on rule-based logic or static optimization models, which are hard to adapt to real-world scenarios with dynamic and uncertain conditions. In this context, designing intelligent, efficient, and scalable charging relocation strategies has become a key technical challenge for achieving a sustainable transportation system.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a large-scale multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for EV fleet management, integrating zone-level autonomous agents with a hybrid decision architecture. The proposed system employs Independent Proximal Policy Optimization (IPPO) with Centralized Training and Decentralized Execution (CTDE), where 50 zone-level agents learn coordinated policies through parameter sharing while maintaining local decision autonomy during execution. Each agent observes local information including regional demand, available vehicles, charging station status, and average battery levels, and outputs continuous preferences for vehicle dispatching, destination selection, and charging scheduling. These high-level preferences are then mapped to executable actions through a Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) module that handles operational constraints such as parking capacity, charging station availability, and vehicle-order matching. This two-level architecture combines the adaptability of reinforcement learning with the precision of mathematical optimization, enabling the system to scale to realistic city-wide scenarios while maintaining computational tractability.
The innovations and contributions of this paper can be encapsulated as follows:
(i) This paper extends the EV fleet management problem from small-scale centralized control (4 zones, 18 vehicles) to large-scale multi-agent coordination (50 zones, 200 vehicles), demonstrating the scalability and effectiveness of zone-level agent decomposition. The proposed IPPO with CTDE architecture leverages parameter sharing to improve sample efficiency while enabling decentralized execution for real-time responsiveness. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to apply multi-agent reinforcement learning with such scale to the integrated EV relocation and charging scheduling problem.
(ii) A novel two-level decision framework is developed by integrating multi-agent reinforcement learning with optimization-based post-processing. The upper level employs IPPO agents to learn adaptive macro-policies (dispatching preferences, charging preferences) from high-dimensional state spaces (251-dimensional global state, 7-dimensional local observations), while the lower level uses MIP to translate these preferences into feasible micro-actions under complex operational constraints (parking capacity, charging station availability, battery SOC limits). This hybrid architecture effectively bridges the gap between learning-based adaptability and constraint-based feasibility, enabling the system to handle both dynamic demand patterns and physical resource limitations.
(iii) Comprehensive experiments are conducted in a simulation environment built on real-world traffic and charging infrastructure data.The results show that the proposed method improves system-wide profitability and vehicle utilization, offering valuable insights for policy design in EV-sharing systems.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the problem statement. Section 3 introduces the RL framework and algorithms used in this study. Section 4 describes the experimental setup and results. Section 5 provides the conclusions and outlines directions for future work.
RELATED LITERATURE
Vehicle Sharing Relocation Optimization
Vehicle sharing is the management process of systematically planning and dynamically adjusting multiple factors such as departure time, service area, and cost objectives. The goal of this process is to achieve efficient resource utilization and maximize overall operational performance. Early studies primarily focused on routing and task allocation for conventional fuel vehicles. It is to minimize operational costs while satisfying diverse customer demand. Zhong et al. (6) addressed the potential impact of driver familiarity on scheduling stability in local parcel delivery. They proposed a two-level relocating strategy that improved long-term system stability while maintaining scheduling efficiency, offering valuable insights into human-centered relocating system design. Chen et al. (7) developed a hierarchical relocation framework that combined rolling optimization with Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) modeling, enabling efficient and robust resource allocation in complex urban mobility scenarios.
However, with the increasing adoption of EVs, their different energy supply methods and operational characteristics have brought new challenges to shared mobility systems. To address energy consumption and charging constraints in EV relocating, Liang et al. (8) proposed a relocation model that integrates power information and the spatial distribution of charging stations. They also designed an enhanced ant colony system with reinforcement-based preselection and pruning mechanisms to achieve efficient EV deployment in practical scenarios. With the advancement of communication and information technologies, real-time decision-making problems are playing an increasingly important role in the economy (9). Among them, relocation optimization has become a popular research topic in fields such as intelligent transportation, logistics distribution, and power systems. The works of Levin (10), Klapp (11), and Hou (12) present numerous case studies. In these problems, researchers often face core challenges such as high-dimensional state spaces, complex constraints, and uncertainties in dynamic environments (13). To address these problems, existing approaches can be broadly categorized into two main classes: traditional optimization algorithms and learning-based methods such as reinforcement learning and deep learning. In the following sections, we introduce these two categories in detail.
Mathematical Optimization-Based Methods
In complex decision-making tasks like vehicle relocation, optimization algorithms are essential for achieving efficient solutions (14). Traditional methods, including linear programming and integer programming, rely on mathematical modeling techniques to obtain optimal solutions under clearly defined constraints (15). For example, Qiao et al. (16) proposes a modified particle swarm optimization algorithm to address vehicle relocation problems with soft time windows, enhancing the algorithm's ability to handle multiple constraints. Park (17) introduces a hybrid genetic algorithm, which demonstrated strong performance in optimizing multiple objectives such as travel time, weighted delay, and fleet size in vehicle relocating. Huang et al. (18) proposed a two-stage stochastic programming model integrating long-term pricing, real-time vehicle relocations, and flexible access trips to address demand-supply imbalance in one-way carsharing under uncertain demand conditions. Wang and Shen (19) addressed route and charging time constraints in electric bus relocation by developing an improved multiple ant colony optimization algorithm. By integrating a bipartite graph matching strategy, their approaches effectively reduce the number of required vehicles while ensuring the feasibility of charging. Huang et al. (20) developed a mixed-integer nonlinear programming model to jointly optimize pricing and departure time incentives in SAV systems, effectively addressing spatiotemporal demand imbalance under uncertainty and revealing both short-term and long-term behavioral impacts.
Traditional optimization algorithms perform well in static relocation problems. However, they often struggle to produce satisfactory strategies in complex scenarios involving dynamic environments, changing demands, and high-dimensional state spaces.
Reinforcement Learning-Based Methods
On the basis of the above, RL, with its ability to learn through interaction, has been widely applied to policy optimization in dynamic relocation systems (21). Mao et al. (22) propose a model-free deep RL framework to address the supply-demand imbalance in taxi relocating. Zhou et al. (23) design a distributed demand relocating method based on Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), significantly improving system scalability and real-time performance. Building on this, Liu et al. (24) introduce a single-agent deep reinforcement learning strategy, named Deep Relocation. This method pre-allocates idle vehicles to potential demand areas in ride-hailing platforms. Both the simulator and policy model are developed using industrial-scale real-world data, and the high-concurrency relocation matching problem are handled using ranking concepts from recommendation systems.
Reinforcement learning has shown great potential in solving dynamic relocation problems across domains, including industrial manufacturing and electric vehicle fleet management, particularly in the context of growing shared mobility demands. In industrial manufacturing, Zhang et al. (25) combine RL methods with digital twin systems to achieve efficient task relocation under spatiotemporal constraints which demonstrate the effectiveness of reinforcement learning in addressing dynamic relocation problems. Zhang and Yang (26) develop the Charging-control Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (CDDPG) algorithm, which integrates Long Short-Term Memory mechanisms and dual experience replay to significantly improve policy stability and learning efficiency. These approaches represent recent advances in applying reinforcement learning to relocation problems
With the continuous advancement of globalization, the demand for transportation and mobility services continues to rise, driving the rapid development of vehicle sharing systems. In this context, RL has gained increasing attention as a promising tool for optimizing EV charging and relocation decisions in shared fleets. However, as the fleet size increases, RL-based methods encounter several key challenges. First, the state and action spaces expand rapidly, making it harder for agents to efficiently explore the environment and converge to optimal policies. Second, large-scale shared mobility systems involve multiple agents. Each vehicle may act as an agent. This raises challenges related to coordination, competition, and non-stationarity. Third, the environment becomes more dynamic and uncertain, with factors such as fluctuating user demand, traffic conditions, and charging infrastructure availability introducing stochasticity into the learning process. Moreover, in EV-based fleets, the decision-making process must also incorporate additional constraints like battery degradation, charging station capacity, and grid load balance, further increasing the complexity of the RL formulation. These challenges show the need for RL algorithms that are scalable, sample-efficient, and robust. Examples include the optimizer-integrated reinforcement learning approach proposed in this paper. Huang et al. (29) proposed a deep reinforcement learning approach for shared autonomous vehicle relocation, comparing car-based and zone-based agent models under multiple network scales, and demonstrated that the enhanced zone-based method significantly improves operational efficiency and system profit. Liu et al. (28) proposed a dynamic clustering-driven multi-agent reinforcement learning framework (DC-MARL) for optimizing the real-time relocation of shared autonomous electric vehicles, effectively mitigating supply-demand imbalance and improving system profitability. These methods help ensure effective learning and real-time use in large-scale, real-world shared mobility systems (27).

Problem statement
Problem statement
This study proposes a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework to address the EV charging and relocating problem. The problem is formulated as a decision-making task under a set of operational constraints, with a particular focus on charging behavior. On one hand, the framework aims to relocate idle vehicles to fulfill ride demands and improve fleet utilization. On the other hand, it considers whether vehicles have sufficient battery levels and directs them to charging zones when necessary.
The core objective is to maximize system profit through real-time decision-making in an uncertain environment. To this end, we design this DRL framework that models the relocation task as a MDP and applies the DDPG algorithm to handle complex policy learning in continuous action spaces. As shown in Fig. 1, the framework jointly considers critical factors such as the spatial distribution of charging stations, vehicle locations, and battery levels. It constructs a composite state space and reward function to guide the agent toward learning efficient relocating strategies.
The entire system is implemented within a high-fidelity simulation environment built on real-world traffic networks and charging infrastructure layouts. This enables a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method in terms of real-time performance, stability, and adaptability, demonstrating its feasibility and potential for practical deployment.

By modeling actions in terms of zonal tendencies rather than precise trajectories, the action space remains compact and suitable for learning in complex, large-scale environments.

State space
The state space is defined by three key features for each zone: the number of ride demands, the number of available vehicle positions, and the number of available charging stations. These elements collectively capture the dynamic operational status of the environment.
Specifically, the number of ride demands reflects real-time demand and helps guide vehicle relocating decisions. The number of available positions represents how many idle vehicles are currently present in each zone, providing insight into supply distribution. The number of available charging stations indicates the charging infrastructure’s capacity and availability, which is crucial for energy-aware relocation.
By including these features across all zones, the state representation enables the agent to perceive spatial and temporal imbalances in demand and supply. This allows for informed decision-making aimed at improving fleet efficiency, reducing customer wait times, and optimizing charging behavior under real-world constraints.

DDPG algorithm
DQN introduces two key mechanisms. First, the experience replay buffer is used to break the correlation between training samples. Second, the target network stabilizes Q-value targets by updating its parameters with a delay. DQN was originally designed to solve decision-making problems in discrete action spaces. In such settings, it is possible to enumerate all possible actions  and select the one with the highest Q-value. However, in continuous action spaces, this  becomes a non-convex optimization problem that is difficult to solve. As a result, DQN is no longer directly applicable.
In this context, DDPG was developed. It retains two key ideas from DQN but introduces a new structure based on the Actor-Critic framework. DDPG adopts a deterministic policy and uses a separate policy network (Actor) to output continuous actions. At the same time, it employs a Critic network to estimate the Q-value of the selected action, which is then used to guide the update of the Actor. Eq. (18) shows the actor network, Eq. Result comparison and analysis
This paper conducts a comparative analysis between rule-based methods and RL-based methods. In the rule-based method, vehicle behaviors are divided into three categories: relocation, order serving, and charging. Relocation is determined by a predefined relocate strategy. When a region has available vehicles and a positive departure tendency, vehicles are reassigned to other regions based on destination preferences. Order serving is triggered when valid OD demands exist and idle vehicles are available in the region. The system matches orders in a cost-minimizing manner. Charging is automatically triggered by vehicle status. When a vehicle is idle and its battery level is below 20%, it is relocated to a region with available charging stations. CONCLUSION
This paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning-based framework to address the EV charging relocation problem. By formulating the problem as a MDP and applying the DDPG algorithm, the proposed method learns adaptive policies to coordinate vehicle relocation and charging decisions. The decision-making process considers both order assignment and battery state constraints, and the reward function is carefully designed to balance service performance and energy efficiency. A post-processing optimization module is also integrated to map continuous actions to feasible relocations under real-world constraints.
To evaluate the proposed framework, a high-fidelity simulation environment is constructed using actual traffic and infrastructure data, simulating vehicle movements and charging behaviors across four parking areas. The system was trained over 1500 episodes and tested on 200 episodes, each capped at 12 steps. The actor and critic networks were optimized using learning rates of 0.0001 and 0.001, respectively, with soft target updates performed every 2 episodes using a smoothing factor of τ = 0.01. Specifically, the learned policy reduces the number of out-of-battery events by 42.86%. The results demonstrate that the learned policy significantly improves system profitability, vehicle utilization, and service rates, compared to traditional rule-based baselines. The DDPG-based model also shows strong generalization capabilities in adapting to spatial-temporal variations in demand and charger availability.
However, this study also presents several limitations. Although reinforcement learning demonstrates strong capabilities in adaptive policy optimization, its training process heavily depends on high-quality data and significant computational resources. Moreover, the current framework optimizes vehicle behavior solely from the system perspective. This omission may limit the model’s ability to reflect passenger satisfaction and adapt to demand-side variability.
